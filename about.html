About

My background is in economics — a BA from Boğaziçi, and two master's degrees since, at LMU Munich and now European Economic Studies in Bamberg — but most of the actual work has been applied: harmonizing cross-country trade data at the World Trade Organization, building inflation and recession-risk models at Allianz Research, and running econometric analysis for two research chairs at LMU along the way.

Lately I've been pulled toward the more technical end of that work — Python, time-series and panel models, and increasingly LLMs and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) applied to economic research rather than just spreadsheets and regressions. I'm currently working toward doctoral research in a quantitatively oriented academic environment, building on this combination of empirical economics and applied machine learning.

Research interests

  • Empirical macro-finance & forecasting

    Inflation dynamics, monetary policy transmission, and recession risk — using VAR, panel/time-series models, and LSTM-based forecasting, as in my master's thesis on inflation surprises and US trade flows.

  • Corporate reporting & sustainability disclosure

    How financial and non-financial information combine to explain firm performance, risk, and market outcomes — and how large structured and unstructured corporate datasets can be analyzed at scale.

  • Textual analysis of financial communication

    NLP-based work on central bank communication and green bond spreads, looking at how textual and sustainability-related signals shape investor expectations and financial outcomes.

  • Applied AI for research

    Retrieval-augmented generation, LangChain/LangGraph, embeddings, and vector databases — exploring how these can complement established econometric methods for large-scale document analysis in economics.
